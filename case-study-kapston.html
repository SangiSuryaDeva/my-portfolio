Kapston Services

Kapston Services is a service-based marketplace with separate mobile experiences for customers and service providers, spanning drivers, watchmen, security guards, and other home or personal services.

Two audiences, two apps

I designed both the customer app and the service-provider app. Separating the experiences made it possible to give each audience a focused interface instead of forcing two different needs into one application.

Customer experience

The customer-side design was framed around making service discovery and selection understandable. The exact production feature set and booking details should be checked against the live app before publishing screen-level claims.

Service-provider experience

The provider-side app was designed as a dedicated experience for the people delivering services. Its exact operational features should be represented using approved original screens or verified reconstructions rather than assumptions.

A connected marketplace

The two apps belong to one service ecosystem but serve different goals. The case study focuses on designing both sides together while keeping their interfaces tailored to their audiences.

Outcome

Two separate mobile applications—one for customers and one for service providers—were designed, and the product is live. Detailed outcome metrics and exact feature-level evidence were not available for this draft.

My contribution

Design of customer and service-provider mobile applications. Specific research methods, feature details, and measured outcomes are not claimed where they have not been confirmed.

Project context

Overview & design challenge.

01 / Overview

A live service marketplace with two mobile experiences: a customer app and a service-provider app, serving drivers, watchmen, security guards, and other home/personal services.

02 / Problem to solve

Customers and providers participate in the same service journey but need different information and actions. The product structure connects the two sides without treating their interfaces as identical.

Design rationale

Key design decisions.

Grounded in the project details available for this portfolio.

Design for two audiences

Separate customer and provider experiences rather than combining different needs into one interface.

Keep the service journey legible

Use a high-level discover/select/request sequence without claiming unverified production features.

Connect the two sides

Emphasize the relationship between customer demand and provider-side work without assuming backend implementation.

Outcome & learning

What this work reinforced

Marketplace UX is a coordination problem as much as a screen-design problem: each side needs clarity, while the overall service journey must connect.
